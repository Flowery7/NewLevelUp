Temporary Roofs in London: Protection That Pays for Itself

Published 18 September 2026 · Level Up Scaffolding

Any roof project — a re-felt, re-slate, chimney rebuild or full re-roof — exposes your home, and your family, to the weather. In London that means rain, wind and the inevitable leak that turns a simple repair into an expensive one. A temporary roof is the answer, and it's one of the most underrated structures we erect. Remember: we build and hire the temporary roof structure itself — your roofer carries out the roofing work on top of it.

What is a temporary roof?

As the name suggests, it's a weatherproof scaffold covering erected above your existing roof while work goes on beneath it. Professionally built from premium-grade materials, it keeps the rain out and lets your roofer carry on in almost any conditions — which is why a temporary roof can mean the difference between a two-week job and a two-month one.

Why it saves you money

  • Keeps work moving — no weather delays means your project finishes sooner.
  • Protects your property — stops water ingress damaging ceilings, floors and furniture.
  • Protects trades — roofers can work safely without slips on wet materials.
  • Reduces risk — less exposure to wind damage on unfinished rooflines.

Is it right for your project?

If your roof is being opened up for more than a few days, or you're working through autumn and winter, a temporary roof is almost always worth it. Our team will survey your property and recommend the right structure for your roof's pitch and size — using only premium-grade materials for all projects. We erect the cover; your roofing contractor does the rest.

Guide · Chimney Stacks

Chimney Stack Repairs: Why Safe Scaffolding Matters

Published 4 September 2026 · Level Up Scaffolding

Chimney stacks sit right at the top of your home, exposed to decades of weather — which is why they're so often the source of damp, leaks and brickwork problems. But repairing one means working at height, and that's where the right access makes all the difference between a quick fix and an accident. We don't carry out the chimney repairs ourselves — we erect the safe, secure scaffold that lets your chimney specialist do the work with confidence.

Why you shouldn't cut corners on chimney access

  • Working at height is dangerous — chimney repairs need a stable, engineered platform, not a ladder.
  • It's high and exposed — stacks are the highest point on most roofs, with no natural handholds.
  • Tools and materials need a solid base — re-pointing and repointing involve heavy, awkward loads.
  • Safe demolition — if a stack needs lowering, proper access protects you, the roof and the street below.

What a chimney scaffold looks like

A well-built chimney scaffold gives your chimney specialist a comfortable, secure working platform right at stack height. Depending on your property it may be a dedicated stack scaffold, a full-height house scaffold, or a part of a combined structure that also covers guttering and roof tiles.

Protecting the surrounding area

Working around a chimney also means protecting the tiles and gutter fittings below you. Experienced fitters protect the roof surface with board protection, and for stacks overhanging a pavement or street, we agree the right site safety measures — including potential highway permits — so everyone stays safe, from your tradesmen to passers-by.

Our approach

As we always say, your satisfaction is our priority, through to execution. We survey, confirm the right structure, quote transparently and install carefully — so your chimney specialist can begin their work on solid, safe ground.

How to Choose a Scaffolding Company in London

Published 21 August 2026 · Level Up Scaffolding

Finding a good scaffold company isn't just about the cheapest quote. The structure we erect carries the people working on your roof — roofers, chimney specialists, solar installers — and it's responsible for the safety of your family, your building and the public outside. Here are the things we'd look for — and the questions we'd ask before hiring anyone.

1. Safety is non-negotiable

Ask directly about training and insurance. Scaffolding and access towers must be installed by trained professionals — check that the crew are qualified scaffolders and that the company carries full insurance. Never be shy about asking for proof; a reputable firm will be happy to show it.

2. Check their work

A portfolio of real projects tells you a lot. Look for tidy, professional structures — consistent fittings, proper stabilisers and clean base plates. If their photos show sloppy work, walk away. Our recent projects show the standard we install.

3. Get a clear, itemised quote

Beware of vague prices. A proper quote covers the structure, access, delivery, any permits and the expected hire period. Watch out for hidden extras later. A transparent quote means fewer surprises at the end.

4. Communication and punctuality

Scaffolding companies turn up when they say they will. Delays disrupt your whole project and your neighbours' street. Choose a team that keeps you informed at every stage — before, during and after installation.

5. Cover a wide area

Local knowledge matters. A company experienced across Central, West, East, North and South London understands the different property types, conservation areas and permit requirements you'll meet across the capital. We cover Greater London and the surrounding suburbs and happily advise on site-specific rules.

Taking a few extra minutes to choose the right company now will save you money, stress and worry later. When you're ready, we'd be glad to quote — our advice is always free, and there's no obligation.
